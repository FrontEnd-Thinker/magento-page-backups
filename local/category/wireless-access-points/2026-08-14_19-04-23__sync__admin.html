Wireless Access Points at a Glance

Wi-Fi 6 and Wi-Fi 6E Performance

Wi-Fi 6 (802.11ax) delivers up to 9.6 Gbps of theoretical throughput with OFDMA and MU-MIMO technology, meaning more devices share the spectrum without stepping on each other. Wi-Fi 6E extends those gains into the 6 GHz band for near-zero interference in dense environments like open offices, auditoriums, and warehouses.

Centralized Cloud Management

Every brand we carry offers cloud-managed or controller-based administration. Push SSIDs, VLAN assignments, and RF channel plans to every AP across every site from a single dashboard. Zero-touch provisioning means new units come online without a technician on-site, and firmware updates roll out silently during off-hours.

Indoor and Outdoor Coverage

Indoor models fit ceiling tiles, open ceilings, and desktop environments. Outdoor and ruggedized units handle parking lots, loading docks, stadiums, and industrial yards with IP67-rated enclosures rated for extreme temperatures. Both form factors are available with PoE+ so a single Ethernet cable handles power and data.

Security and Firewall Integration

Access points from SonicWall, Fortinet, and WatchGuard integrate natively with the matching firewall platform, applying user-aware policies, segmenting guest traffic into isolated VLANs, and feeding wireless events into the same security dashboard as wired traffic. No separate wireless controller license is required on most platforms.

Wireless Access Points FAQ

A managed wireless access point is a business-grade Wi-Fi radio that is configured and monitored from a central controller or cloud dashboard rather than through a local web interface on each unit. This means you can push configuration changes, update firmware, and view usage reports across every AP in your network from one place, which matters the moment you have more than two or three access points.

Wi-Fi 5 (802.11ac) operates on the 2.4 and 5 GHz bands and handles most business workloads well. Wi-Fi 6 (802.11ax) adds OFDMA scheduling and BSS Coloring, which dramatically improve performance in high-density environments where many devices connect simultaneously. If your environment has 25 or more concurrent users per AP, or you run latency-sensitive applications like VoIP or video, Wi-Fi 6 is worth the step up.

A general rule for open office environments is one AP per 1,500 to 2,500 square feet, but that number changes significantly based on ceiling height, wall construction, concurrent device count, and the applications running on the network. Dense environments like conference rooms, classrooms, or warehouses with metal shelving need a more specific site survey. Most business-class access points are powered over Ethernet (PoE or PoE+), which means they draw power from the network switch rather than from a wall outlet. You will need a PoE-capable switch or a PoE injector for each AP. Wi-Fi 6 APs with 2.5 Gbps uplink ports typically require PoE+ (802.3at) or PoE++ (802.3bt) rather than the older 802.3af standard.
