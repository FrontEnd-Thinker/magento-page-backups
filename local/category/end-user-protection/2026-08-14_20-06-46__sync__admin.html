End User Protection at a Glance

Endpoint Detection and Response

EDR goes beyond signature-based malware detection to monitor behavior across every process, file write, and network connection on the endpoint. When something looks suspicious, it records the full attack chain, isolates the device automatically, and gives your team the forensic trail needed to understand exactly what happened and what else to check. Sophos Intercept X and SonicWall Capture Client both include EDR capabilities.

Ransomware Rollback

Sophos Intercept X includes CryptoGuard technology that detects ransomware encryption in real time and automatically rolls back affected files to their pre-attack state, limiting data loss to seconds rather than hours or days. This feature alone has reversed ransomware attacks for hundreds of Firewalls.com customers without paying a ransom or restoring from backup.

Centralized Cloud Management

Every endpoint solution we carry is managed from a cloud console, meaning no on-prem management server to maintain. Deploy agents remotely, push policy updates to every device in seconds, view real-time threat status across the entire organization, and generate compliance reports from one place. Multi-tenant dashboards are available for MSPs managing multiple clients.

Managed Detection and Response

If your team does not have the bandwidth to monitor endpoint alerts around the clock, Sophos MDR provides a 24x7 team of threat hunters who investigate alerts, contain threats, and notify you of critical findings. MDR turns the software into a fully managed service without requiring additional headcount on your side.

End User Protection FAQ

EPP (Endpoint Protection Platform) focuses on prevention: blocking malware, exploits, and malicious websites before they execute. EDR (Endpoint Detection and Response) adds continuous monitoring and response capabilities, recording what happens on the endpoint so threats that slip past prevention can be detected, investigated, and contained. Most enterprise solutions today combine both in a single agent. If you can only choose one tier, go with EDR, as prevention alone is no longer sufficient against modern threats.

Yes. A firewall protects traffic at the network perimeter, but it cannot see what happens after a file lands on a device, an employee opens a malicious email attachment, or a remote worker connects from an unsecured network. Endpoint protection covers the device itself, catching threats that enter through email, USB drives, browser downloads, or encrypted traffic that the firewall cannot inspect. The two layers work together, not as substitutes for each other.

Ransomware rollback detects when ransomware begins encrypting files and automatically restores the affected files to their pre-encryption state using shadow copies maintained by the endpoint agent. Sophos CryptoGuard, included in Intercept X Advanced and above, is the most well-known implementation of this feature. It works independently of your backup system, meaning files can be recovered in seconds even if the backup was not recent. It is not a substitute for backup, but it is a powerful last line of defense when ransomware executes.

Most cyber insurers now require next-generation endpoint protection with EDR capabilities as a baseline condition for coverage. Basic antivirus is no longer sufficient. Some insurers also require MDR or 24x7 monitoring as a condition for ransomware coverage specifically. If you are renewing or applying for cyber insurance, ask your broker for the specific technical controls they require and we can match you to a solution that satisfies them.

Endpoint protection is typically licensed per seat, per year. A seat is one device, whether a workstation, laptop, or server. Some vendors offer bundles that cover a mix of device types under a single license tier. Multi-year terms are usually available at a discount. Firewalls.com can quote single-year and multi-year pricing side by side so you can see the long-term cost before committing.
