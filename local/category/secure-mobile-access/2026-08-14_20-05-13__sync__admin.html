SonicWall Secure Mobile Access FAQ

SonicWall Secure Mobile Access is a purpose-built SSL VPN and zero-trust remote access platform. It gives employees, contractors, and partners secure, role-based access to corporate applications and resources from any device or location. SMA appliances enforce granular access policies, multi-factor authentication, and device posture checks, and integrate with SonicWall firewalls to extend your security perimeter to every remote session.

The main differences are user capacity and hardware redundancy. The SMA 6210 supports up to 250 concurrent SSL VPN users and is designed for SMBs and branch offices. The SMA 7210 scales to 500 concurrent users and adds redundant power supplies, making it the better fit for mid-market and enterprise deployments where uptime is critical. Both run the same SonicOS software and share the same management interface.

Yes. The SMA 8200v is SonicWall's virtual appliance option. It deploys on VMware ESXi, Microsoft Hyper-V, KVM, Amazon Web Services (AWS), and Microsoft Azure, and carries the full SMA feature set. Capacity is determined by the user licenses you assign rather than fixed hardware limits, so you can scale sessions as your workforce grows.

Capture ATP (Advanced Threat Protection) for SMA is a cloud-based, multi-engine sandbox service that scans files transferred through your SMA gateway in real time. It is designed to block ransomware, zero-day exploits, and advanced malware that traditional signature-based antivirus misses. If your remote users routinely transfer files over the VPN, Capture ATP adds a meaningful layer of defense without adding latency to the appliance.

Each SMA appliance ships with a base concurrent-user count. You purchase additional user licenses to raise that limit. Licenses are available as 1, 2, or 5-year subscriptions in packs of 5, 10, 25, 50, 100, or 250 users. The appliance enforces the licensed concurrent-session count; once the cap is reached, new session attempts are queued or denied depending on your configuration.

A pooled perpetual SMA license never expires and lets you allocate user capacity across multiple SMA appliances from a single shared pool. Unlike subscription licenses, there is no annual renewal cycle. Pooled perpetual licenses are a cost-effective choice for organizations with stable, predictable remote access headcounts that want to minimize recurring licensing overhead.

Yes. SonicWall SMA supports zero-trust principles through its policy engine, which grants access to specific applications and resources rather than full network access. Combined with device posture checking, multi-factor authentication, and least-privilege access controls, SMA enforces a deny-by-default model that limits lateral movement even if credentials are compromised.
