Network Switches at a Glance

PoE Power Delivery

Power over Ethernet lets a single cable deliver both data and power to access points, IP cameras, VoIP phones, and IoT devices, eliminating the need for local power outlets at each endpoint. PoE+ (802.3at) delivers up to 30W per port and covers most access points and cameras. PoE++ (802.3bt) handles higher-draw devices like PTZ cameras and next-gen Wi-Fi 6 APs.

VLAN and Network Segmentation

Managed switches let you divide a single physical network into multiple logical segments using VLANs, isolating guest Wi-Fi traffic from internal systems, separating IoT devices from workstations, and grouping servers into their own broadcast domain. Segmentation limits the blast radius of a breach and simplifies compliance for PCI, HIPAA, and similar frameworks.

Stacking and Redundancy

Stackable switches connect together through dedicated high-speed backplane links and appear to the network as a single logical unit. This means you can expand port count without redesigning your network, apply a configuration change to all switches simultaneously, and survive the failure of one unit without losing connectivity on the others.

Unified Cloud Management

SonicWall, Fortinet, and Meraki switches integrate into the same cloud management platforms as their firewalls and wireless products, giving you a single pane of glass for wired and wireless infrastructure. Port-level monitoring, VLAN changes, and firmware updates push out remotely without a site visit or console cable.

Network Switches FAQ

An unmanaged switch plugs in and works immediately with no configuration. It is fine for simple connectivity needs but offers no visibility or control over what is happening on the network. A managed switch lets you configure VLANs, monitor port-level traffic, set QoS priorities, enable spanning tree, and receive alerts when something goes wrong. For any business environment, a managed switch is the right choice.

Power over Ethernet (PoE) delivers electrical power through standard network cable, eliminating the need for separate power adapters at each device. If you are deploying wireless access points, IP cameras, VoIP phones, or door-access readers, you need PoE ports or a PoE injector at each device. PoE+ (802.3at) is the most common standard and covers most APs and cameras. Count your powered devices before buying to make sure the switch's total PoE budget meets your needs.

A Layer 2 switch forwards traffic based on MAC addresses and operates within a single network segment. A Layer 3 switch adds IP routing capability, meaning it can route traffic between VLANs internally without sending that traffic to a separate router or firewall. Layer 3 switching reduces latency for inter-VLAN traffic and is recommended for larger networks with multiple segments that need to communicate with each other at high speed.

The switch connects to the firewall's LAN port through a trunk link that carries all your VLANs. The firewall handles inter-VLAN routing and applies security policies between segments. If you purchase a switch from the same brand as your firewall, the management integration is native and you can manage both from the same dashboard. Our configuration service handles the full setup, including VLAN tagging, trunk configuration, and firewall policy updates, for a flat fee.

Count every wired device you need to connect today, then add 20 to 30 percent for future growth. Include workstations, access points, IP cameras, phones, printers, and servers. Remember that some of those ports will be PoE ports. Common sizes are 8, 16, 24, and 48 ports. If you exceed what one switch can handle, stackable models let you add capacity without replacing the core unit.
