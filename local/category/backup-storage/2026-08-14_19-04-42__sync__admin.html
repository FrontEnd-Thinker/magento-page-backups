Backup and Storage at a Glance

Automated Backup Scheduling

Effective backup runs automatically on a defined schedule without manual intervention. Modern backup solutions protect servers, workstations, virtual machines, and SaaS data with incremental forever or continuous data protection (CDP) approaches that capture changes every few minutes rather than nightly. Automated backup verification tests each recovery point so you know backups are actually restorable before you need them.

Offsite and Cloud Replication

A backup stored only at your primary site is vulnerable to the same disaster that hits your production data, whether that is a fire, flood, or ransomware attack that encrypts local storage. Offsite replication sends copies to a secondary location or cloud storage automatically, satisfying the 3-2-1 rule: three copies of data on two different media types with one copy offsite. Cloud replication targets from Barracuda and other vendors are built in and require no separate infrastructure at the secondary site.

Bare-Metal and Virtual Recovery

Bare-metal recovery restores a failed server to completely different hardware without requiring the original machine to be available, by abstracting the OS and application layer from the underlying hardware. Virtual machine recovery spins up a protected server as a VM in seconds so users can keep working while physical hardware is repaired or replaced. Both capabilities are critical for minimizing downtime after a hardware failure or ransomware event.

Ransomware-Resilient Architecture

Ransomware frequently targets backup systems alongside production data to maximize leverage. Immutable backup storage uses write-once retention policies that prevent any process, including ransomware, from modifying or deleting recovery points during the retention period. Air-gapped cloud copies and role-based access controls that separate backup administration from general IT administration further reduce the risk that an attacker can destroy your recovery options before you know an attack is underway.

Backup and Storage FAQ

The 3-2-1 rule means keeping three copies of your data, stored on two different types of media, with one copy offsite. For example: production data on your servers, a local backup copy on a backup appliance or NAS, and a replicated copy in the cloud. This strategy ensures that no single failure, including a fire, hardware failure, or ransomware attack, can destroy all copies of your data simultaneously. Many cyber insurance policies and compliance frameworks reference the 3-2-1 rule as a baseline expectation.

Backup refers to creating recoverable copies of data. Disaster recovery (DR) is the broader process of restoring business operations after a disruptive event, which includes recovering data from backup but also covers infrastructure recovery, failover, and the procedures your team follows to get systems back online. A backup appliance is a tool. A disaster recovery plan defines the recovery time objective (RTO) and recovery point objective (RPO) that tell you how fast you need to recover and how much data loss is acceptable.

Microsoft 365 replicates data across its own infrastructure for availability, but this is not the same as backup. Microsoft's service-level agreements cover infrastructure uptime, not data recovery from accidental deletion, ransomware encryption, or malicious insider activity. Deleted items are recoverable for a limited window after deletion, and that window can be shortened or eliminated by ransomware that deliberately purges the recycle bin. A dedicated Microsoft 365 backup solution retains independent copies of Exchange, SharePoint, OneDrive, and Teams data with longer retention and granular recovery options.

Immutable backup storage uses a write-once policy that prevents any user, application, or process from modifying or deleting a recovery point during its defined retention period. Even an administrator with full credentials cannot alter or delete an immutable backup until the retention period expires. This protects recovery points from ransomware that specifically targets backup systems and from accidental or malicious deletion by internal users. Both on-prem and cloud backup targets can be configured with immutability.

At minimum, you should verify that backup jobs complete successfully every day and perform a full test restore of critical systems at least quarterly. Automated recovery verification built into modern backup platforms performs synthetic restore tests against each recovery point and alerts on failures without requiring manual intervention. Annual or semi-annual tabletop disaster recovery exercises that walk through the full recovery procedure are also recommended to validate that your team knows what to do and that the documented process actually works.
